Transportation integration

1 · Physics-constrained influence functions [S1] → trustworthy traffic state estimation. The advance (abstract level): measure how much each training example changes a constrained network model, while respecting the network's physical constraints (for example flow conservation at nodes). Problem it changes: when a traffic estimate looks wrong, agencies cannot tell which detector caused it; bad loops and stuck sensors silently distort corridor estimates. Mechanism: classic influence functions approximate the effect of removing one training point through a Hessian-weighted gradient; constraining that calculation to the feasible set (conservation, nonnegative densities) stops it from blaming or crediting physically impossible adjustments. Simplest credible test: a CTM-simulated freeway with 20 synthetic detectors; corrupt 2 (stuck-at-zero, +30% bias); fit a conservation-constrained estimator; rank detectors by influence. Baselines: leave-one-detector-out refitting (expensive but exact) and a simple residual-outlier rule. Metric: rank of the two corrupted detectors; agreement with leave-one-out. Effort: about 20 hours after the Residual Lab. What could make it fail: approximation breaks for highly nonlinear (congested) regimes; constraints not active where the fault is. Who cares: TMC operators, detector maintenance teams. Verdict: Try now, after the Residual Lab; it is the natural sequel to the verifier you are building.

2 · Signal control: max-pressure review [S3] and Traffic-VL [S2]. The advance: a consolidated view of max-pressure, a decentralized controller with a throughput-optimality result under its queueing assumptions, plus a vision-language controller that reportedly matches but does not beat a structured-state learner [S2], following earlier VLM controllers [S9]. Problem: adaptive control is expensive to deploy; agencies need methods that run on existing controllers with detector data they already have. Mechanism: max-pressure picks the phase with the largest sum of (upstream queue − downstream queue) weighted by saturation flow, so it reacts to spillback without a network model. Simplest credible test: a 4×4 grid in SUMO; compare fixed-time, actuated, max-pressure and an RL controller under normal demand and a lane-blocking incident; metric: average delay and throughput at saturation. Effort: about 15 hours once SUMO runs. What could make it fail: queue measurement error (max-pressure needs queues, most detectors give occupancy); RL overfits the training demand. Who cares: city signal engineers, controller vendors. Verdict: Try now as a learning project; Watch the VLM line, because a camera-to-signal model adds cost without a demonstrated gain over structured state.
