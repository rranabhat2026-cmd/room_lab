Transportation integration

Three items developed fully; the rest get one line. Verdicts: Try now / Watch / Not for transportation yet.

1 · SS4A FY26 awards → safety analysis. The advance: about $1.013B across 297 awards, 100 of them implementation grants [S1]. Problem it changes: hundreds of agencies must now rank locations and later show that countermeasures worked, and the default practice of ranking by raw crash counts overstates sites that had an unlucky year (regression to the mean). Mechanism: Empirical Bayes (HSM Part B) shrinks each site's observed count toward a safety-performance-function prediction, weighted by how reliable each is. Simplest credible test: Florida intersections in Palm Beach County (crash records through a Florida crash data portal such as Signal Four Analytics, if your university access allows), split by years (rank on 2021–2023, evaluate on 2024–2025); baselines: raw counts (required), EB with a published SPF, then a gradient-boosted model; metric: how many of the top-20 sites stay in the top 20 out of sample, and the Poisson log-likelihood of future counts. Effort: about 15–20 hours once data access works. What could make it fail: access or geocoding quality, SPFs not calibrated to Florida, too few years. Who cares: cities that just won awards, their consultants, FDOT safety offices. Verdict: Try now.

2 · Tool-using LLMs driving planning engines → verified planning. The advance: an LLM edits and runs an equilibrium assignment through a tool interface [S9]; a DOT case study drafts work-zone plans [S10]. Problem: an agency cannot trust a scenario if nobody checks that the edit the LLM made is the edit the planner asked for. Mechanism: Wardrop user equilibrium has an independent optimality check, the relative gap, so a separate verifier can recompute it and confirm link changes against the request (PASS / FAIL / UNKNOWN). Simplest credible test: the Sioux Falls benchmark network in AequilibraE; 30 natural-language scenario requests with known correct edits; inject 10 corrupted edits (wrong link, wrong direction, capacity units). Baseline: LLM with no verifier. Metric: corrupted-edit catch rate and false alarms, plus relative gap below 1e-4 on accepted runs. Effort: about 20 hours. What could make it fail: requests too ambiguous to define a correct edit; verifier only as good as its spec. Who cares: MPO modelers, planning software vendors. Verdict: Try now; it is also the cleanest transportation home for EvidenceOS.

3 · Koopman-operator prediction and ramp metering → freeway control [S11], with 2-D model calibration as related context [S12]. The advance (title-level only): lifting nonlinear traffic dynamics into polynomial observables where they evolve approximately linearly, so linear prediction and control become available. Problem: model predictive ramp metering needs a model that is fast and good enough; first-principles LWR/CTM models need calibration, while deep models are opaque. Mechanism: LWR conservation ∂ρ/∂t + ∂(ρ·v(ρ))/∂x = 0 is nonlinear; EDMD fits a linear operator on chosen observables (ρ, ρ², …), and the truncation error depends on how well those observables close the dynamics. Simplest credible test: a cell-transmission-model freeway with one on-ramp and an incident scenario; compare EDMD-with-control against persistence and an ARX model for 5–15 minute prediction, then Koopman-MPC against ALINEA (the standard local feedback metering law) on total delay. Effort: about 25 hours; builds directly on the Verified Physics Residual Lab. What could make it fail: the observables do not capture shock formation; gains disappear under a misspecified (incident) regime. Verdict: Watch; read the paper before investing, but schedule it as your PIML bridge project.

P5 · Transportation systems practitioner. Worldview update: implementation grants mean real countermeasures, and agencies will need defensible evaluations in HSM terms [S1]. Terms: HSM Part B and C; SPF calibration factor; crash modification factor (CMF) and its standard error; CMF Clearinghouse; systemic safety; ALINEA; TSMO. Deprioritize: methods that need vehicle trajectories agencies do not collect. Warning: if an AI method cannot output expected crashes per year with uncertainty, an agency cannot use it.

Real disagreements: P2 wants to move on SS4A tooling now while the money is fresh; P1 wants you to master EB and evaluation design first. P3 sees LLM-driven planning as the opportunity; P4 thinks small linear models are the deployable win; both can be tested cheaply and neither should be assumed.
