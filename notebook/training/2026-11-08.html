Recognize

  • Max-pressure: choose the phase with the largest weighted sum of upstream minus downstream queues.
  • Saturation flow: the maximum discharge rate of a movement during green, in vehicles per hour of green.
  • Spillback: a downstream link fills up, so vehicles cannot leave the intersection even on green.
  • Actuated control: green times extend or end based on detector calls; the realistic baseline for any new controller.
  • Occupancy: fraction of time a loop detector is covered; not the same as queue length.
  • Influence function: first-order estimate of how a model changes when one training point is removed.

Understand · Max-pressure in one intersection

Intuition: give green to the movements that would move the most vehicles from a crowded place to an empty place. If the downstream link is full, sending vehicles there achieves nothing.

Formulation: for a movement from link i to link j with saturation flow s, its weight is s × (q_i − q_j), where q is the queue. A phase's pressure is the sum of its movements' weights. Max-pressure picks the phase with the largest pressure at each decision step.

Worked example (checked; all s equal to 1): Phase A serves N→S with queue 12 (downstream queue 4) and S→N with queue 8 (downstream 6): pressure = (12 − 4) + (8 − 6) = 10. Phase B serves E→W with 10 (downstream 2) and W→E with 5 (downstream 9): pressure = (10 − 2) + (5 − 9) = 4. Choose Phase A.

Notice the negative term: W→E pushes vehicles into a fuller link, so it lowers Phase B's pressure. That is how max-pressure reacts to spillback without a network model.

Why it matters: it is decentralized, needs only local measurements, and has a throughput-optimality result under its queueing assumptions [S3]. Limitation: it needs queue lengths; most detectors report occupancy, and it ignores coordination and pedestrian phases unless added. Simpler alternative: well-tuned actuated control is often enough at isolated, under-saturated intersections.
